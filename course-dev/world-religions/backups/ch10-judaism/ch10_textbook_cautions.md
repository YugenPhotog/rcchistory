# Ch10 Judaism: textbook cautions (instructor note)

For Steven Austin, PHIL 210 / REL 210 World Religions. Prepared 2026-10-19. This note is for the instructor only and is **not** in the L1–L3 decks.

Six statements in the Chapter 10 textbook reading (printed pp. 221–252) are wrong, imprecise, or out of date. Each textbook quote below was checked verbatim against the PDF text. Each correction was checked on 2026-10-19 against the source named.

| # | Page | Textbook says (verbatim) | Problem | Check / source | In the decks? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 225 | "a nine-branched menorah is used for the nine days of the festival" | **Error.** Hanukkah lasts eight days. The ninth branch holds the helper light (*shamash*), which is used to light the others. | Babylonian Talmud, Shabbat 21b (William Davidson Edition, Sefaria): Rava says "One must kindle another light in addition to the Hanukkah lights in order to use its light." | L1 V popup says "eight days"; L2 AP notes carry a caution. |
| 2 | 230 | "Rome brought the end of every group in Judaism except the Pharisees" | **Oversimplified.** On the same page the textbook says it took "at least two centuries" for the rabbis to win authority. Other groups declined over time; they did not vanish in 70 CE. | Textbook p. 230 itself. | L1 X notes say Rome "eliminates or weakens several Second Temple movements". |
| 3 | 245 | Shabbat "concludes on sunset on Saturday" | **Imprecise.** Traditional practice ends Shabbat at nightfall, when three stars appear, with Havdalah. | Shulchan Arukh, Orach Chayim 293:2 (Sefaria Community Translation): no work "until he sees three small stars". | L3 X says "nightfall on Saturday, when Havdalah ends it"; the X notes carry a caution. |
| 4 | 247 | "In all Orthodox practice, women are not permitted to participate in rituals such as these, so a female's ceremony is usually little more than a modest party to mark her thirteenth birthday" | **Dated and sweeping.** In traditional law a girl comes of age at 12, not 13. Orthodox bat mitzvah practice today varies by community. *I did not verify current Orthodox practice against a source.* | Mishnah Niddah 5:6 (Sefaria): a girl's vows bind from twelve years and one day, a boy's from thirteen years and one day. | L3 AB says "12 for girls in many communities"; L3 AD says practice varies by movement. |
| 5 | 251 | "Reform Judaism doesn't believe that the Torah is divinely written or inspired" | **Contradicted by the movement's own platform.** Reform Judaism denies verbal dictation, but it affirms that the Torah is revelation. | CCAR, *A Statement of Principles for Reform Judaism* (Pittsburgh, 1999): "We cherish the truths revealed in Torah, God's ongoing revelation to our people and the record of our people's ongoing relationship with God." | Not taught in L1–L3. |
| 6 | 235 | "Adolf Hitler and his Nazi Party won office in the 1932 German national elections" | **Imprecise.** The Nazis became the largest party with 37% in July 1932. Hitler was not elected; he was appointed chancellor on January 30, 1933. | United States Holocaust Memorial Museum, Holocaust Encyclopedia: Hitler "was not directly elected to power". | Not taught in L1–L3 (it is in the rabbit-hole supplement's era). |

## Flagged, not verified
- **p. 247:** "Orthodox Jews affirm the Talmudic view that a person of Jewish descent who is not circumcised will not enter heaven." This looks doubtful as a general statement of Orthodox belief. I did not check it against a source, so it is listed for your review only.

- Items 5 and 6 bear on Reading Guide Q4 (Reform) and the modern history pages. Students answer Q4–Q5 from the textbook, pp. 233–234 (L1 AE), so you may want to flag item 5 when you assign them.
