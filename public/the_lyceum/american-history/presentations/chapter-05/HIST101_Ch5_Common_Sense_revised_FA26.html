Declarations of Dependence
◆Initially, colonists viewed themselves as subjects of the Crown who claimed English liberties, not Americans struggling against a foreign aggressor.
◆Refused to contemplate, demand, or declare independence
◆Traditional liberties under attack; English law on their side
◆Most still hoped for reconciliation; a general war would look like a civil war inside the empire.
◆As of late 1775–early 1776, most colonial instructions still forbade a vote for independence.
Literature of Revolution
◆The pamphlet was the decisive genre: cheap to produce, portable, read aloud, and shared.
◆It carried the live political argument of the day, not just news.
◆Direct replies to events sparked chains of polemics.
Wider print ecology: newspapers, almanacs, magazines, and broadsides.
Pamphlets carried the best thought of the day and all elements of American political thought.
Pamphlet content varied widely; commemorative orations were another form.
Direct replies produced chain-reacting personal polemics.
Letters from a Farmer in Pennsylvania
◆Written by John Dickinson, lawyer and statesman.
◆Twelve letters, serialized in 1767–68, then published as a pamphlet.
◆The most widely reprinted political pamphlet in the colonies before Common Sense.
◆Accepted British regulation of trade; opposed using tariffs to raise revenue.
◆He argued revenue tariffs were unconstitutional and destructive to liberty.
Speaking cues: Dickinson voiced opposition to the Townshend Acts; his argument became a common line of reasoning.

Galvanizing Force of Thomas Paine
◆One of the greatest political propagandists in history.
◆His formal education was meager.
◆Benjamin Franklin advised Paine to go to America and gave him letters of introduction.
◆Arrives in Philadelphia on November 30, 1774.
His formal education was meager, just enough to enable him to master reading, writing, and arithmetic.
At 13 he began work with his father as a corset maker and then tried various other occupations unsuccessfully, finally becoming an officer of the excise. His duties were to hunt for smugglers and collect the excise taxes on liquor and tobacco

Arrived deathly ill with typhus (80 out of 125 passangers sick). Lay in bed for six weeks before full recovery.
Made connections with Richard Bache, married to Benjamin Franklin’s daughter. Introduced him to Robert Aitken, printer and publisher.
Paine met Benjamin Rush browsing the bookshelves in Aitken’s book shop. After reading Paine’s article on slavery, Rush became better acquainted with paine.

Speaking cue: after several unsuccessful occupations, Paine became an excise officer.
Common Sense: publication and reach
◆Benjamin Rush urged Paine to write a pamphlet but avoid the word “independence.”
◆Common Sense published January 10, 1776.
◆Paine claimed 120,000 copies in three months. We cannot audit that number. What we can say: the pamphlet was reprinted across the colonies, read aloud, and borrowed—reach, not a barcode count.
Speaking cue: Paine began work for one of the first magazines published in America, the Pennsylvania Magazine.
Common Sense: a radical work
◆Truly radical work.
◆Changed the terms of debate.
◆Attacked the ideological infrastructure of reconciliation thought.
◆Destroyed the assumptions the next three slides examine.
Assumptions of Reconciliation Destroyed by Common Sense

Assumption 1

Rights, law, and justice flowed from government.

Common Sense

  • Paine insisted rights were natural rights granted by Creator not government
  • Paine popularized and weaponized natural-rights and social-contract arguments against reconciliation.
  • Changes terms of debate: Britain had to defend its record protecting and preserving those rights

Paraphrase: Monarchy was an institution rooted in superstition, dangerous to liberty, and inappropriate to Americans.

Assumptions of Reconciliation Destroyed by Common Sense

Assumption 2

The quarrel was with Parliament and colonial ministers, not the king.

Common Sense

  • Paine attacks King personally
  • Oppose not just tyranny, but the tyrant
  • Shattered bond between America and Great Britain

“the Royal Brute of Britain.”

Speaking cue: Paine portrayed the king’s conduct as a perversion of a father’s expected role.
Assumptions of Reconciliation Destroyed by Common Sense

Assumption 3

America depended on Great Britain for trade and protection.

Common Sense

  • Cutting ties with war-prone England would strengthen American security
  • Trade would receive an immediate boost; American goods always have a market in Europe
  • Foreign aid only will come with declaration of independence

Markets with which America hath enriched itself are the necessaries of life, and will always have a market while eating is the custom of Europe.

Paine explored benefits of separation.
Ye that love mankind…
◆Independence could address grievances and secure self-government for the nation and future generations.
◆Paine presented this situation as unprecedented.
“ We have it in our power to begin the world over again. A situation, similar to the present, hath not happened since the days of Noah until now.”
An asylum for mankind
O! ye that love mankind! Ye that dare oppose not only tyranny but the tyrant, stand forth! Every spot of the Old World is overrun with oppression. Freedom hath been hunted round the globe. Asia and Africa have long expelled her. Europe regards her like a stranger and England hath given her warning to depart. O! receive the fugitive and prepare in time an asylum for mankind.
Speaking cues:
Opportunity transcends founding a new nation.
Unique destiny never before presented in history.
Opportunity to fight for American liberty and liberty itself.
How public pressure changed the vote
◆Widespread grassroots success helped push colonial legislatures to change their instructions to Congress.
◆Farmers now had a cause they would fight for: liberty.
◆Not only to end a king, but to found a republic ordinary people could own.
What the war is for
◆The shooting war is already a year old (Lexington, April 1775). Paine changes what the war is for: from constitutional protest over taxation and rights toward independence and a new political order of liberty and self-government.
◆Common Sense removed the last psychological barrier and helped make the Declaration speakable.
Speaking cue: people awakened by Common Sense.
Context: Common Sense paved the way for the Declaration of Independence.
Paine shifted the terms of debate and helped make independence speakable; he did not supply the Declaration's wording.
