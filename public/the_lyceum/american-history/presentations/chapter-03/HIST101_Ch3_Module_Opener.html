HIST 101 · U.S. History I · Chapter 3
A Frightened Continent
Faith, War, and Belonging, 1670–1720

For twenty years, the severed head of a Native leader sat on a pole above the town of Plymouth — a trophy from a war that nearly destroyed New England.

A thousand miles west, an entire European empire was driven out of its own colony and kept out for twelve years.

In a Massachusetts village, terrified people began accusing their neighbors of witchcraft — and hanged twenty of them.

And a seven-year-old minister’s daughter, carried off in a winter raid, grew up, was offered the chance to come home — and said no. For the rest of her life.

These four stories are all the same story. This unit is about what connects them.

The Big Idea

Most of us learn early American history as a story of thirteen English colonies growing along the Atlantic coast, with "the frontier" somewhere off at the edge. This unit asks you to see it differently.

Between roughly 1670 and 1720, North America was not yet a European continent with a Native edge. It was overwhelmingly Native ground, and the handful of European colonies clinging to its coasts and river valleys were far weaker, far more frightened, and far less in control than their own confident writings claimed.

The Throughline of the Whole Unit
Indian power set the terms.

You will see that one sentence proven four different ways — through a war, a revolt, a witch hunt, and a captivity. Watch for it. It will return at the end of every lecture, and by the last one you should be able to explain it yourself.

How This Unit Works

The textbook gives you the facts. The lectures give you the meaning. Your chapter reading covers the who, what, and when. The four lectures don’t repeat that — they dig into why it matters and how it all connects. You need both.

Each lecture is built around one big question and returns to it at the end. Each has short moments where you’ll stop and think, write a sentence, or make a prediction — those aren’t busywork, they’re where the learning sticks.

One lecture (Salem) pairs with an assigned video you must watch first. Watch for the due date — that lecture assumes you’ve already seen it.

The Four Lectures

One arc, escalating from war on the outside to a choice made on the inside. The emotional register shifts as you go: holy war → total war → terror turned inward → crossing over.

LECTURE 1 · TOTAL WAR
The War That Made Colonists Savage
Metacom’s War (King Philip’s War), 1675–76. A confident New England is brought to the brink of destruction — and left afraid of what the fighting has turned it into. We start with a severed head on a pole and work backward to ask how it got there.

“Now the God of the Spaniards Is Dead”
The Pueblo Revolt, 1680. In New Mexico, the Pueblo peoples expel an entire European empire — and keep it out for twelve years. Then we stop and ask a hard question: how do we even know why they did it, when the only records were written by the people they defeated?

As You Go Through This Unit, Watch For…

  • The recurring throughline. "Indian power set the terms" returns at the end of every lecture. Notice how each story proves it differently.
  • Fear as a thread. Every lecture is, at heart, about fear — where it points, what it does, and what happens when it has nowhere safe to go.
  • Causes are usually plural. This unit trains you to stop asking "what was THE cause?" and start asking "how much did each cause matter?"
  • A continent, not a coastline. By the end, try to see early America as many peoples’ ground — not thirteen colonies with an empty frontier at the edge.
