## Notes on image selection

This deck deliberately uses only **2 major visuals** — fewer than L1 (4) or L2 (3) — a direct response to the L2 audit. Several image searches for this lecture surfaced material that had to be rejected:

- **No portrait of Edward Braddock exists from before his death.** Every available image (an 1899 engraving by William Sartain, 19th-century "Braddock's Defeat" illustrations, Leutze-style history paintings) was created decades or over a century later, several showing anachronistic details (one popular illustration, *Washington at the Battle of the Monongahela*, depicts British troops in Revolutionary War–era uniforms from a war fought twenty years later). Given the L2 error, none of these were used. Braddock is referenced entirely through text and the popup definition, which itself notes the absence of any surviving contemporary likeness.
- **No period-verified image of Fort Beauséjour or the Acadian deportation was confirmed in the time available.** Rather than risk a second dating error on a topic this sensitive, the Acadia section (slides 18–22) is built entirely through text and statistic treatment, consistent with the skill's guidance that a source-heavy, historiographically careful lecture may use fewer, slower slides rather than reaching for decorative imagery.

Both omissions are deliberate and documented here rather than silently absent. A future revision pass could search specifically for a verified period map of Nova Scotia/Acadia (e.g., a properly catalog-checked Des Barres or Morris survey) to give the Acadia section visual parity with the Ohio Country section.
