# Delivery Report

## Essential question
How did the Great Awakening become a struggle over who could claim legitimate spiritual authority in eighteenth-century British America?

## Narrative engine
Jonathan Edwards, George Whitefield, and Gilbert Tennent carry the lecture by showing how revival moved from inward conversion to public controversy over who was fit to speak for God.

## Coverage triage
### Core classroom concepts
- Great Awakening as crisis of authority
- revival and conversion
- Edwards's theology and restrained teaching style
- Whitefield and transcolonial revival
- Tennent and the challenge to clerical authority
- New Light / Old Light conflict
- institutional and social consequences
- historiographical debate over the Revolution connection

### Deferred to Independent Study
- deeper historiographical dispute over the unity of the Awakening
- Whitefield and slavery in fuller detail
- regional unevenness across colonial America
- vocabulary review slide

### Omitted
- dense scholar-by-scholar literature review beyond Heimert, Bonomi, Johnson, and Butler
- detailed denominational chronology beyond what first-year students need live

## Chosen narrative architecture
Transformation / escalating conflict: comfortable religious world → revival roots → revival/conversion/disruption → Edwards → Whitefield → Tennent → denominational conflict → consequences → historiographical interpretation.
