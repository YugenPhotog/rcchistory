## Notes on image selection

This deck uses only **2 major visuals**, continuing the increasingly selective pattern across this arc (L1: 4, L2: 3, L3: 2, L4: 2) as image-search discipline tightened after the L2 audit. Two searches were deliberately not pursued given time constraints and risk:

- **A Pontiac portrait.** Following the same pattern as Braddock in Lecture 3, essentially all available imagery of Pontiac is a later invention — 19th-century engravings and paintings made long after his death, none from life. Given no time was available to verify a specific file's date and identity to the same standard applied elsewhere in this arc, Pontiac is handled entirely through text and the popup definition, which itself notes the absence of any contemporary likeness and the caution that he did not command a single pan-Indian army.
- **A Peace of Paris territorial map.** A map showing the 1763 boundary changes would have been a natural visual for slide 15, but verifying a properly dated, non-anachronistic period or clearly-labeled modern reference map was not completed in the time available. The territorial terms are instead conveyed entirely through text, which — given the complexity of the actual settlement (the public Peace of Paris plus the separate secret Treaty of Fontainebleau) — arguably needed the precision of text more than the simplification a map would have imposed anyway.

Both gaps are documented here rather than silently absent, consistent with the practice established in the L2 and L3 manifests.
