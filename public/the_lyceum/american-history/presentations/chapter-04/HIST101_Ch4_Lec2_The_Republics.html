The French Called Them "Republics"

They meant it as an insult.

Why would an empire insult a village by calling it self-governing?

This Was Not Empty Land

  • Shawnee communities in the upper Ohio watershed
  • Delawares, pushed steadily west for a century
  • Senecas and other Haudenosaunee, claiming authority over the region
  • Miami communities, and Great Lakes peoples with their own histories of displacement
None of them were waiting for Europeans to organize their affairs.

The French System Was Breaking Down

How it worked

  • Gifts, mediation, ritual reciprocity
  • French officials as "fathers" — with real obligations attached

By the 1740s

  • Louisbourg's fall interrupts trade goods for years
  • Veteran diplomats die or leave office
  • Replacements give orders instead of negotiating

Logstown and Its Sister Villages

  • Great Lakes peoples, further displaced

Kinship Was the Security Policy

Every village held relatives of every nearby nation.
To attack Logstown was to attack someone's cousins — who lived in villages you might need tomorrow.

"It Needed No Explanation"

Duquesne — governor of New France from 1752 — sends 2,000 Canadians to build a line of forts in 1753, so convinced the policy benefits Indians that he never explains it to them.
  • The Mingoes warn him not to build in their territory
  • Delawares and Shawnees ask Virginians: will you expel the French and then go home?

The Ohio Company of Virginia, 1747

  • Chartered on a 1609 sea-to-sea claim
  • Sets out to survey and settle the valley

A Correction Worth Making

Your textbook says the Ohio Company hired George Washington as its surveyor.

It hired Christopher Gist, commissioned in 1750. Gist mapped the valley across two expeditions. Washington's tie was family: his brother Lawrence became a company director after founder Thomas Lee's death. Washington himself went west in 1753 as the governor's emissary, not the company's surveyor.

Textbooks compress. This is what checking looks like.

A Fifth Party: The Chain Itself

  • The Covenant Chain — the metaphor governing Haudenosaunee–British relations
  • Alliances require continuous renewal — gifts, ceremony, grievances addressed
  • Left untended, the Chain tarnishes. Tarnished enough, it breaks.

Hendrik

  • Theyanoguin — Mohawk sachem
  • Decades navigating British–Haudenosaunee diplomacy
  • By 1753 — deeply frustrated
A British print, London, 1755 — sold within months of Hendrik's death, based on an earlier, now-lost portrait

"We Desire to Hear No More of You"

The Covenant Chain is broken between you and us. So brother you are not to hear of me any more, and Brother we desire to hear no more of you.
— Hendrik (Theyanoguin) to the Governor of New York, 1753

Interpret

A British printmaker made this — likely after Hendrik's death. Its own caption calls him both "in Alliance with" and "Subject to" the King.
What is this print claiming about Hendrik and imperial authority — and how does that clash with the ultimatum he actually gave?

Albany, 1754

  • Britain orders New York to host an intercolonial congress
  • Purpose: redress Haudenosaunee grievances — and find a plan for coordinated defense

Franklin's Snake

  • May 1754, Pennsylvania Gazette
  • A President General, a Grand Council
  • Shared taxation, shared defense, shared Indian policy
Pennsylvania Gazette, May 9, 1754

Adopted at Albany. Rejected Everywhere Else.

The Congress adopts Franklin's plan. Every colonial legislature then rejects it — most with little debate, some unanimously.

Two Explanations. Which Has More Power?

A. The colonies didn't yet see themselves as one people.

B. The plan threatened real local power — patronage, taxation, military command — for uncertain collective gain.

Who Controlled the Valley?

Nobody European. The French label was an insult — and an accidental admission: neither empire actually governed these towns.

A Caution About the Word

  • The Delawares were not pursuing Anglo-American "liberty"
  • They wanted trade on fair terms, respected land, and honored kinship obligations
Coherent claims on their own terms — not a rough draft of Jefferson.

The Colonies Enter This War Divided

No shared command. No shared plan. A Chain just declared broken.

Independent Study

The Middle Ground and the Plan That Almost Was

For the online hour.

Richard White's "Middle Ground"

  • A space where neither side could simply impose its will
  • French officials as "fathers" — obligated to provide, protect, and adjudicate
  • Not equality. Genuine reciprocity, within limits.
White's framework has been debated since 1991 — it remains essential, but it is not beyond challenge.

Inside the Albany Plan

  • A President General, appointed by the Crown
  • A Grand Council, elected by colonial legislatures for three-year terms
  • Authority to tax, raise armies, and manage Indian affairs across all colonies
Some features foreshadow later federal government — but this was an imperial plan, with a Crown-appointed executive, not an early draft of the Constitution.

Hendrik at Lake George

  • 1755 — Hendrik dies fighting alongside British and provincial forces
  • The same alliance his 1753 ultimatum nearly ended
A man who told the empire where it stood — then died holding the line anyway.

Mingoes

Iroquois people — primarily Seneca and Cayuga — who relocated westward into the Ohio Country during the 18th century, settling outside the direct authority of the Haudenosaunee Confederacy's core territory in New York.

Their move gave them independence from Confederacy oversight, but also meant British colonial officials sometimes questioned whether Confederacy leaders could speak for them.

The Covenant Chain

The diplomatic metaphor through which the Haudenosaunee Confederacy and the British colonies managed their relationship from the 17th century onward — a bond that required continuous renewal through ceremony, gift exchange, and grievance resolution.

Left untended, the Chain "tarnished." Tarnished enough, it broke — which is exactly what Hendrik declared to New York in 1753.
