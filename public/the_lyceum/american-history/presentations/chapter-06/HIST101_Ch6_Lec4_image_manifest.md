## `Scene at the Signing of the Constitution of the United States.jpg`

- **Source URL:** `https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Scene_at_the_Signing_of_the_Constitution_of_the_United_States.jpg`
- **Slide use:** Title slide (full-bleed background) and returned to on the penultimate classroom slide
- **Category:** Later historical representation — Howard Chandler Christy, 1940, U.S. Capitol
- **Verification status:** Verified
- **Interpretive caution:** **Load-bearing for the deck's architecture.** The cold open asks students to name the painting's mood; the closing slide reveals the 1940 sesquicentennial commission and treats "calm, orderly, inevitable" as choices the painter made. Never present it as evidence about 1787.

## `Daniel Shays and Job Shattuck.jpg`

- **Source URL:** `https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Daniel_Shays_and_Job_Shattuck.jpg`
- **Slide use:** "The Courts Were the Target" (content + image layout)
- **Category:** Contemporary primary-source visual — woodcut, *Bickerstaff's Boston Almanack* for 1787
- **Verification status:** Verified
- **Interpretive caution:** **Hostile source, and the caption says so.** The artist almost certainly never saw either man; the woodcut dresses farmers as Continental officers with swords, a flag, and a cannon in order to portray the Regulation as an organized military conspiracy. Excellent for teaching that a contemporary image can be evidence of *rhetoric* rather than of appearance.

## Verification method

Every Commons filename below was confirmed against its live `File:` description page before
being written into a deck. Filenames were never guessed or reconstructed from memory.

All `<img>` elements carry an `onerror` fallback to `.img-placeholder`. Title-slide background
images cannot take `onerror`, so each title slide also declares
`data-background-color="#1A1A1A"`, which means a failed load degrades to the house charcoal
rather than to white.

**Note on network:** this container cannot reach `commons.wikimedia.org` (egress allowlist),
so the URLs could not be fetched here. They were verified by description page, not by HTTP
response. Worth one click-through on first classroom use.
