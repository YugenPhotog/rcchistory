The revised slide-face principle is explicit in the file header: **each brief phrase identifies its subject and teaches why it matters**. Unfamiliar people receive a role or relationship, and dates or names are joined to their consequence. For example, “Li Linfu’s death · 752/753” became “Chief minister Li Linfu protected An at court” followed by “Li’s death left An unprotected.” “Feng Changqing’s hurried recruits” became “Feng Changqing’s new recruits could not match An’s veterans.”

Five inline SVGs show the winter advance, Tong Pass corridor, Lingbao battlefield, Suiyang supply corridor, and capital recoveries. These are labeled schematics, not scale maps or reconstructed tactical surveys. There are no external images or fonts.

Slides 4, 6, 8, 13, and 16 use an even two-column composition: four historical teaching points on the left and a half-width schematic on the right. The schematic labels were enlarged for the reduced map area.

## Historical audit

The narrative was checked against Shao-yun Yang's recent Cambridge synthesis, David A. Graff's published research, and relevant entries of the *Zizhi tongjian*. The table records the factual checks and the qualifications retained in the notes.

**Textbook limitation:** Ebrey's actual Chapter 5 pages were not supplied and were not found among the available files. Page 85 (and the host's 84–85 locator) is inherited from the existing lecture. The deck explicitly labels added campaign detail. No claim is made to have independently verified Ebrey's wording, or to have discovered a specific disagreement in an unseen passage. The major population correction is stated directly without attributing an unverified error to Ebrey.

## Spoken-cue check and severability

All slide-face cues were passed through English speech synthesis at its normal rate and checked for length. Years were expanded into normal spoken forms. Long wording was shortened and the final run completed successfully: every measured cue was 4½ seconds or less. The longest was the course / chapter metadata at 4.50 seconds; the longest substantive teaching phrase was 4.39 seconds. This meets the approximate four-second target with a few near-threshold labels. This is a synthesized read-aloud check, not a human pronunciation check or full live rehearsal. Source footers were treated as citations rather than spoken teaching cues.

A scratch copy was made by literally deleting the first two slide sections and their reading-view duplicates. It starts with “A favorite without a protector”; its opening narration names An Lushan, Xuanzong and Yang Guifei and establishes their relationship without relying on the deleted setup. The remaining 20 slides render and navigate; the first displayed folio resets to 01. The delivery copy retains both setup slides, marked with the exact requested severable-setup comment.

## Compression and pacing

The faces now preserve both the evidence and its immediate significance: dates, estimates, names, places, roles, decisions, and consequences. Full explanations of motives, conflicting army totals, pass geography, the accession sequence, the Suiyang sources, alliance costs and the census caveat remain in connected podium prose. Battle casualty totals and disputed sensational siege numbers were omitted. Lesser campaign maneuvers and the long institutional aftermath were compressed out. There are no activities, presenter stage directions, or developed discussions of the fubing system and postwar governance.

The notes average about 114 words per budgeted minute, leaving space for natural emphasis and attention to maps. Actual delivery will vary. The final HTML comment contains the per-slide timing budget and running total.
