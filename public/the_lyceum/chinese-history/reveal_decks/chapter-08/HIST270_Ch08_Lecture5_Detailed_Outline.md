## Historical argument

Foot binding changed over centuries: origin stories, Song elite evidence, wider Ming–Qing diffusion, varied ethnic and household practices, and later reform are parts of one developing history. Beauty, erotic meanings, status, family expectations, and labor all belong in its explanation. A closing comparison helps students examine social pressures on bodies while distinguishing differences in age, power, harm, and permanence.

**Framing question:** How can we explain a culturally valued practice, recognize its harm, and compare it responsibly with familiar body practices?

## Learning targets

- Distinguish an origin legend from evidence that a practice existed.
- Explain the Song case and the practice’s later development without treating all periods alike.
- Connect bodily consequences with beauty, family resources, and differing social meanings.
- Recognize ethnic and regional variation and the limits of simple policy explanations.
- Explain how reform and changing economic circumstances contributed to decline.
- Use a cultural comparison to identify both a parallel and a consequential difference.
