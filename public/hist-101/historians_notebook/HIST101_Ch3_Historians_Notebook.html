Chapter Inquiry

How did Native power shape the terms of survival, fear, and belonging across a continent colonists did not control?

How the lectures build

The chapter begins where English settlement looked most secure, then crosses to Spanish New Mexico, turns frontier fear inward at Salem, and ends with captives whose adult lives crossed the boundaries colonists tried to defend. Together, the lectures test a continental claim: Native nations constrained colonial power through war, revolt, diplomacy, kinship, and belonging.

The lecture map

  1. 1. The War That Made Colonists SavageA regional war exposes the fragility—and moral anxiety—of English New England.
  2. 2. Now the God of the Spaniards Is DeadPueblo communities expel Spanish rule and expose the limits of imperial and archival authority.
  3. 3. The Devil in the Shape of an IndianA frontier-war interpretation of Salem is tested against a genuinely multicausal crisis.
  4. 4. The Captives Who Wouldn’t Come HomeCaptive lives reveal porous borders and forms of belonging beyond Puritan New England.

Lecture 1

The War That Made Colonists Savage

Guiding question

Why did a society that nearly collapsed in war keep Metacom’s head on public display for roughly twenty years?

Argument to Watch For

Metacom’s War revealed that English control remained contingent, and colonists used providence and public memory to defend a moral boundary their own violence had destabilized.

Selective notes

At this stage

Track how land loss, colonial jurisdiction, praying towns, and competing Native strategies narrowed peaceful options without turning Metacom into the commander of one unified Native side.

Fragments are fine.

Write One Sentence · Individual · 1–2 minutes

Could peace still protect Wampanoag autonomy?

Metacom says conversion would make him “poor and weak.” Name one peaceful strategy that might still have protected autonomy in 1675—and why it was unlikely to work.

Selective notes

At this stage

Record how mobility, ambush, the Great Swamp Fight, Native alliances, and colonial adaptation explain both early Native success and the war’s eventual turn.

Arrows encouraged.

Quick Write · Individual · 1–2 minutes

What does providence conceal?

Increase Mather calls the war God’s judgment on a sinful people. What does that explanation let colonists avoid asking about their own conduct?

Predict · Individual · 1–2 minutes

Before the memory reveal

How do you predict New England will publicly remember this war a generation later?

Selective notes

At this stage

Capture the qualified scale of destruction, the enslavement and displacement of Native survivors, and the political or emotional work performed by Metacom’s displayed head.

Future You forgets fast.

Closing takeaway

English victory depended on Native allies, colonial adaptation, fractured coalitions, and severe violence; the pike turned a frightening, many-sided war into a durable story of one defeated enemy.

Lecture 2

Now the God of the Spaniards Is Dead

Guiding question

How did Pueblo communities expel Spain for twelve years, and what can a coerced record honestly tell us about why?

Argument to Watch For

Religious persecution, material crisis, and coordinated Pueblo action destroyed Spanish rule, but victory did not create one Pueblo future or a transparent historical record.

Selective notes

At this stage

Track how drought, disease, raiding, forced labor, religious persecution, and the 1675 punishment of medicine men made ceremonial autonomy inseparable from survival.

Questionable spelling welcome.

Write One Sentence · Individual · 1–2 minutes

Why call it sorcery?

The Spanish prosecuted Pueblo practice as sorcery—not rebellion or labor refusal. What does that charge reveal about what Spanish authorities believed was at stake?

Selective notes

At this stage

Note the coordination and scale of the 1680 revolt, twelve years without Spanish colonial government, and why success against Spain did not produce permanent unity under Po’pay.

Scribble zone.

Quick Write · Individual · 1–2 minutes

Read a coerced source carefully

The surviving declarations came through interrogation by the defeated Spanish authorities. What deserves caution, and what information may still be usable?

Predict · Individual · 1–2 minutes

Who drives the horse story?

Native communities already acquired, bred, traded, and integrated horses before 1680; Spain’s expulsion redistributed more animals. How does that correction change who appears to drive the history?

Selective notes

At this stage

Record how interrogation shapes the archive and how the revolt redistributed animals and may have accelerated Native-controlled horse networks without originating them.

Park anything worth keeping here.

Closing takeaway

The revolt made Native power unmistakable, while its divided aftermath and compromised sources warn against turning victory into unity or testimony into a transparent transcript.

Lecture 3

The Devil in the Shape of an Indian

Guiding question

How much explanatory weight can frontier war bear in an account of Salem?

Argument to Watch For

Wabanaki war, refugee trauma, and military failure shaped Salem, but only in interaction with local feuds, gender and religious expectations, political instability, and spectral evidence.

Selective notes

At this stage

Track Norton’s claim, the timing of the Wabanaki war, and why frontier violence belongs in the explanation without replacing every other cause.

Write it down before it evaporates.

Evidence Judgment · Individual · 1–2 minutes

Which witness will carry more weight?

Before examining both cases, choose whether Mercy Lewis or George Burroughs will provide stronger evidence that frontier war shaped Salem. Commit to a reason.

Quick Write · Individual · 1–2 minutes

Choose the verb

Was Salem “caused by” the frontier war or “shaped by” it? Choose the more accurate verb and defend it in one sentence.

Selective notes

At this stage

Separate the documented legal and political mechanisms of prosecution from the plausible—but not directly documented—interpretation that military frustration redirected fear toward reachable internal enemies.

A few words can do real work.

Connect Back · Individual · 1–2 minutes

One fear, a later form

Lecture 1 showed colonists fearing that war had made them savage. How is Salem a continuation of that fear rather than a wholly new crisis?

Closing takeaway

Frontier war belongs in Salem’s explanation, but the strongest account is multicausal and distinguishes emotional context from the legal mechanisms that made accusation lethal.

Lecture 4

The Captives Who Wouldn’t Come Home

Guiding question

What can the documented adult lives of Eunice Williams and Esther Wheelwright reveal about captivity, evidence, and belonging?

Argument to Watch For

Forced childhood capture and later belonging are different historical facts; repeated adult actions can document durable lives beyond New England even when the archive cannot recover inner motives.

Selective notes

At this stage

Distinguish Eunice’s adoption and family life at Kahnawake from Esther’s movement through Wabanaki, French mission, and Ursuline communities.

Notes can be scrappy.

Write One Sentence · Individual · 1–2 minutes

Hold two truths at once

Eunice was taken by force as a child. Her adult actions repeatedly sustained her life at Kahnawake. What is the difference between those statements, and why does it matter?

Selective notes

At this stage

Note whose words the archive preserved, what Eunice’s silence reveals about that archive, and what her repeated actions can establish more securely than claims about motive.

Evidence first; mind-reading later.

Quick Write · Individual · 1–2 minutes

What can silence prove?

What can Eunice’s silence reveal about the archive—and what can it not prove about her inner life?

Selective notes

At this stage

Track how adoption, kinship, Catholic institutions, visits, refusals to relocate, and returns to Kahnawake expose the social as well as military limits of colonial power.

Last box—keep the useful bits.

Connect Across the Series · Individual · 1–2 minutes

How close was the feared world?

Salem hunted the Devil amid fear of the frontier world. Eunice built her adult life within it. What do those responses reveal together?

Closing takeaway

The border was porous: colonial societies could lose people as well as battles, and careful historians can document durable belonging without pretending to hear a silent subject’s private thoughts.
