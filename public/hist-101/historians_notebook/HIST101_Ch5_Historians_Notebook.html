Chapter Inquiry

How did a crisis over governing an empire teach colonists to resist, claim liberty, and build alternative authority?

How the lectures build

The chapter begins with Britain’s effort to govern the territory it won, then follows the growth of counterpower, the widening uses of liberty, and the transfer of effective authority to revolutionary institutions. The arc moves from reform, to resistance, to contested claims, to government.

The lecture map

  1. 1. Can Britain Govern What It Won?Victory creates incompatible fiscal, territorial, diplomatic, and constitutional expectations.
  2. 2. How Do You Defeat a Law Without Holding Office?Colonists turn argument into counterpower by withdrawing the people and cooperation enforcement required.
  3. 3. Who Gets to Use the Word Liberty?A portable political language travels beyond the constitutional limits Patriot leaders intended.
  4. 4. When Does Resistance Become Government?Committees and congresses move from obstructing royal rule to making and enforcing rules themselves.

Lecture 1

Can Britain Govern What It Won?

Guiding question

Could Britain govern its new empire without violating the expectations that had helped it win?

Argument to Watch For

British reform addressed real postwar burdens, but no policy could cheaply reconcile imperial revenue, colonial consent, settler expansion, and Indigenous sovereignty.

Selective notes

At this stage

Track debt, garrisons, administration, diplomacy, and the difference between London’s continuing obligation and assemblies’ memory of negotiated wartime contributions.

Start with the problem.

Pause & Reflect · Individual

What lesson does London draw?

You are a British minister in 1764. Pontiac’s War has exposed the interior. What lesson do you draw—and what will your solution cost?

Selective notes

At this stage

Distinguish what ministers, assemblies, settlers and speculators, and Native nations expected; note why Pontiac did not command one centralized army and what the war proved about effective sovereignty.

Contradictions belong here.

Selective notes

At this stage

Record how the Proclamation regulated rather than permanently ended settlement, and how Sugar, Currency, Quartering, customs, and Stamp measures transferred discretion toward London.

Keep the chain visible.

Pause & Predict · Individual

The governing dilemma

A strong policy violates provincial expectations; a deferential policy fails to govern the claimed empire. What happens next?

Closing takeaway

The postwar crisis grew from several plausible but incompatible projects, not from taxation alone or from an inevitable march toward revolution.

Lecture 2

How Do You Defeat a Law Without Holding Office?

Guiding question

How can people who lack sovereign office make an imperial law stop operating?

Argument to Watch For

The Stamp Act was practically nullified before repeal because resistance deprived it of personnel, compliance, economic support, and legitimacy.

Selective notes

At this stage

Track consent, internal taxation, virtual representation, Dulany’s geographic asymmetry, and why petitions could define a grievance without disabling stamped paper.

Constitutional shorthand is fine.

Pause & Predict · Individual

Petitions failed. What next?

Parliament will not receive your petitions. What move could change the cost of enforcement—and what would it cost your community?

Selective notes

At this stage

Record how resignations, courts, printers, juries, and noncooperation made the law fail; distinguish targeted pressure from destructive violence and note why crowd legitimacy remained contested.

Mechanisms over slogans.

Selective notes

At this stage

Track how nonimportation recruited British interests, moved politics into households, required surveillance, and how print and committees made local resistance continuous and reproducible.

Connect the moving parts.

Closing takeaway

Counterpower emerged in the gap between legislation and administration, but its effectiveness depended on coercion that Patriots had to distinguish from the arbitrary power they opposed.

Lecture 3

Who Gets to Use the Word Liberty?

Guiding question

What happens when groups excluded from formal power apply revolutionary liberty to their own grievances?

Argument to Watch For

Liberty linked many claims without resolving them; its portability widened political action while exposing conflicts among property, hierarchy, slavery, and Native sovereignty.

Selective notes

At this stage

Track the movement from rights of English subjects toward natural rights, and how interest and ideology worked together across different regions and social positions.

Catch the changing meaning.

Universal language and political membership were not the same thing: the Revolution multiplied claims whose outcomes depended on unequal power.

Lecture 4

When Does Resistance Become Government?

Guiding question

When does organized resistance become a transfer of sovereignty?

Argument to Watch For

Resistance became government when alternative institutions began regulating trade, raising force and revenue, administering order, and claiming obedience before independence formally named the change.

Selective notes

At this stage

Track the difference between preventing a government from acting and supplying rules, enforcement, finance, militia, and legitimacy after royal authority weakens.

Define the threshold.

Build a Test · Individual

Is Worcester a revolution?

Write the test you would use: what must be true before resistance counts as a change of government?

Selective notes

At this stage

Record how Massachusetts institutions and the Continental Association linked continental decisions to local committees, public sanctions, consumption rules, and coercive enforcement.

Rules need machinery.

Pause & Reflect · Individual

Is this liberty?

The movement now regulates funerals, entertainment, and pantry goods. What makes this different from Parliament’s regulation—if anything?

Selective notes

At this stage

Track how provincial congresses exercised broad emergency power and how war, Common Sense, local declarations, and the Declaration justified and internationalized an institutional transformation already underway.

Texts arrive after practice.

Closing takeaway

The Revolution changed government before it issued its most famous statement about government, and its first institutions were participatory, regulatory, and coercive at once.
