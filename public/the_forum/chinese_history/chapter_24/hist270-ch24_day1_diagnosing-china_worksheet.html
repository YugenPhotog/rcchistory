Diagnosing China

Between 1895 and 1911, Chinese thinkers asked why China was weak and what would make it strong. Read what four of them said, then fill in the chart.

Part 2 · about 17 minutes

Video: China in Revolution

We watch a classroom excerpt (about 12 minutes) from the documentary China in Revolution, 1911–1949 (PBS, 1989): the warlords and the foreign powers, and then a preview of next class (May Fourth, Sun Yatsen, and the march north). The speakers are eyewitnesses remembering in 1988, so listen for whose memories these are. The video stops twice with a pause slide: at Pause 1, answer five quick questions; at Pause 2, one short question. At the end, Professor Austin tells how the story ends.

Video Pause 1
Five quick questions on what you have seen so far. Click your answer: the first click counts and shows whether you are right, with a short explanation.

1. According to the film, why were warlords able to take power in the early years of the republic?
2. What does the eyewitness who grew up in Guangxi province remember from his childhood?
3. The film says the warlords were “a diverse group.” Which of these warlords does it describe?
4. In the film, what were “concessions”?
5. An eyewitness says foreign countries wanted to divide up China “just like cutting a melon into pieces.” What does he mean?
