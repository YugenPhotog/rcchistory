Part 1 · about 18 minutes

The Iron House

In 1922 the writer Lu Xun told how he gave up medicine for literature, and why he almost refused to write at all. Read his story in his own words, then answer the six short questions. Every answer is in the reading.

Read in class (about 8 minutes): “The Iron House: Lu Xun, Preface to Call to Arms (1922)” ↗, Part 1 (Sendai, Japan) and Part 2 (Beijing).

Then answer here (about 10 minutes): one sentence each, with the paragraph number where you found it, e.g., (Preface, 1.4).

Reading 1: The Dream
When Lu Xun went to medical school in Japan, what did he plan to do after he graduated?

Reading 2: The Slide
How did the faces of the Chinese crowd on the lantern slide look? Quote a few words.

Reading 3: The Decision
After that day, what did Lu Xun decide was China’s “first task”?

Reading 4: The Tool
What did Lu Xun believe was the best way to do that task?

Reading 5: The Iron House
In Lu Xun’s image, what will happen to the people asleep inside the iron house?

Reading 6: The Turn
After his friend answered, what did Lu Xun agree to do?

Part 2 · about 17 minutes

Video: May Fourth 1919

We watch our course video, “May Fourth 1919: China’s Students Take to the Streets” (about 17 minutes). The video stops itself twice with a question card. When it stops, type your answer below.

Video Pause 1
At the peace conference, which country was given Germany’s old rights in Shandong?

Video Pause 2
Besides students, name one group in Shanghai that went on strike in June 1919.

Part 3 · about 3 minutes

Exit Ticket

Exit Ticket
In one or two sentences: Why do historians call May Fourth a turning point? Give one piece of evidence from the video.
