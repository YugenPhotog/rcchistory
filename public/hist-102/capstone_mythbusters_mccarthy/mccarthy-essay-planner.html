What You Are Building

You researched all five individuals in Part 1. Your essay does not need to discuss all five. Choose the strongest 2 to 3 cases or lines of evidence for your argument.

Your standard full essay target is 600 to 900 words. Longer is acceptable when the extra length strengthens the argument rather than repeating it.

Each of the three required body paragraphs should include at least one direct quotation from archival evidence, identify the source, and explain whether that evidence supports, contradicts, or complicates the claim being evaluated.

Step 1 - Thesis

Step 1Thesis Statement

Write one clear, arguable sentence. Your conclusion may be that McCarthy's accusations were substantially correct, largely unfounded, mixed, or more complicated than either simple interpretation.

Step 2 - Introduction

Step 2Introduction Paragraph

Build the introduction in four moves: (1) establish the conventional interpretation of McCarthyism; (2) introduce McCarthy's central accusation; (3) state your thesis; (4) preview the 2 to 3 cases or lines of evidence you will use.

Summarize how McCarthyism is commonly understood and why the controversy matters. Do not call an interpretation a "myth" before you have evaluated it.

Briefly explain what McCarthy alleged in 1950. Keep McCarthy's own accusation distinct from later evidence and later investigations.

Name the specific 2 to 3 cases, cables, or archival findings that will carry your argument. You researched five people, but you do not need to use all five in the essay.

Step 3 - Body Paragraphs

Write three required body paragraphs. Each paragraph should make one analytical point, present direct archival evidence with enough citation information to locate it, and explain what that evidence means. The McCarran and counterargument paragraphs are optional.

Optional Body Paragraph 4Comparing the Tydings and McCarran Committees Optional but recommended
Historiographical Question: The Tydings Committee and McCarran Committee reached different conclusions about communist infiltration and security concerns. Compare what each committee investigated, what each concluded, and what evidence or political circumstances may have shaped those conclusions. Then decide how much weight each investigation deserves in your argument.

Step 4 - Conclusion

Step 4Conclusion Paragraph

Restate your thesis in new words, identify your 2 to 3 strongest findings, and explain what the evidence adds to our understanding of McCarthyism and Soviet espionage.

Step 5 - Works Cited

Step 5Source List

You do not need to memorize a citation style for this assignment. Your goal is source traceability. Give enough information for another reader to locate every source you used, and use the same pattern consistently.

What to include: For a Venona cable, record the cable number or identifying information, date, sending office when available, and URL. For other sources, include the author or organization, title, date if available, page or document reference when relevant, and URL.

Step 6 - Reflection

Step 6Final Reflection

Describe one specific moment in your research when a source surprised you, confused you, strengthened your view, or caused you to revise it. Name the person and source. Three to five sentences is enough.

Step 7 - Full Essay Draft

Step 7Assemble Your Complete Essay

Copy your introduction, three required body paragraphs, any optional paragraphs you use, and your conclusion into one continuous essay. Smooth transitions and remove repeated sentences. Your standard target is 600 to 900 words; longer is fine when it improves the argument.

Assembly order: Introduction - Body Paragraph 1 - Body Paragraph 2 - Body Paragraph 3 - Optional McCarran Paragraph - Optional Counterargument Paragraph - Conclusion. Your Works Cited / Source List remains separate.
