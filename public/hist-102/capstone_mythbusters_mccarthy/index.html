The Historical Question

The Problem You Will Investigate

McCarthyism is commonly associated with reckless accusation, fear, and abuse of political power. At the same time, later declassification of Venona intercepts and Soviet archival material established that genuine Soviet espionage networks operated in the United States during the 1930s and 1940s.

Your task is to investigate five individuals connected to McCarthy's accusations and reach your own evidence-based conclusion. For each case, ask whether the archival record supports McCarthy's accusation, contradicts it, or complicates it.

Competing Evidence and Investigations

The Senate's Tydings Committee and the later McCarran Committee reached different conclusions about communist infiltration and security concerns. Venona documents and the Vassiliev Notebooks added evidence that investigators in 1950 could not fully access.

Rather than assuming that one investigation, one politician, or one later archive settles the entire question, compare the evidence and explain where it leads you.

How the Assignment Works

Step 1Research All Five Individuals

Complete all five case entries in the Research Notes Form. Record the person's government role, Soviet code name when one is documented, McCarthy's accusation, archival evidence, citations, and your assessment of the evidence.

Three cases provide direct archival starting points. Two require more independent research. Use credible primary sources, government archives, scholarly publications, library databases, and the starting links provided.

When all five entries are complete, export the form as a .txt file. This first export is the file you will take to NotebookLM in Step 2. Do not submit it to Canvas yet.

Open Research Notes Form
Step 2Generate the NotebookLM Briefing Doc

Create a new Google NotebookLM notebook and upload your exported Research Notes .txt file as the only source. Use NotebookLM's Briefing Doc feature to generate a structured summary from your research.

Copy the complete Briefing Doc and paste it into the designated field at the bottom of the Research Notes Form. Paste the Briefing Doc as generated. Do not use NotebookLM to rewrite your research notes, your thesis, or your essay.

After pasting the Briefing Doc, export the Research Notes Form again. This second export is the final Research Notes file that you will submit.

If NotebookLM is unavailable or the Briefing Doc feature does not work, contact Professor Austin instead of substituting another AI tool.

Step 3Build the Essay Planner and Full Draft

Use your completed Research Notes to build the essay. You researched all five people, but your essay does not need to discuss all five. Select the strongest 2 to 3 cases or lines of evidence for your argument.

Your standard essay target is 600 to 900 words. A longer essay is acceptable if the extra length improves the argument rather than repeating points.

  • A clear thesis that answers the historical question.
  • An introduction with historical context, McCarthy's claim, your thesis, and a road map.
  • Three evidence-based body paragraphs. Each should include at least one direct quotation from archival evidence, identify the source, and explain how the evidence supports, contradicts, or complicates the claim you are evaluating.
  • A conclusion, works cited, and brief reflection.
  • The full assembled essay draft in the final field, not only the planner scaffolding.

Citations: You do not need to memorize a citation style. Make each source traceable. For a Venona cable, include the cable number or identifying information, date, sending office when available, and URL. For other sources, include enough author, title, date, organization, and URL information for another reader to locate the source. Be consistent.
