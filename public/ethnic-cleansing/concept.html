Core Definition

Ethnic cleansing is the systematic forced removal of an ethnic, racial, or religious group from a geographic area through violence, intimidation, forced deportation, or other coercive means, with the intent to create an ethnically homogeneous region. It is distinct from genocide in that the primary goal is removal rather than complete physical destruction (though the two can overlap).

Where this definition comes from: the United Nations Commission of Experts on the former Yugoslavia wrote in its first interim report (S/25274, 1993) that “‘ethnic cleansing’ means rendering an area ethnically homogenous by using force or intimidation to remove persons of given groups from the area.” The commission repeated this in its Final Report (S/1994/674, para. 129). The course definition above builds on that idea. Ethnic cleansing is not a separate crime in international law; its acts can be prosecuted as crimes against humanity.

Key Elements

  • Systematic: Organized and deliberate, not random violence
  • Forced Removal: Displacement through coercion, not voluntary migration
  • Group Targeting: Based on ethnic, racial, or religious identity
  • Territorial Goal: Creating homogeneous space by eliminating diversity
  • State/Militia Power: Requires organized force to implement

Yugoslav Wars Context

The presentation explores in detail how the term emerged from the Yugoslav Wars (1992-1995), where Serbian forces conducted systematic campaigns of violence against Bosnian Muslims and Croats. The Srebrenica massacre of July 1995—where over 8,000 Bosniak men and boys were systematically executed—represents the moment ethnic cleansing crossed into genocide.
