### ch02-explain-001 — Explain / cause

Which explanation best accounts for England's move toward permanent colonization?

- A. Religious refugees created a single, unified demand for colonies.
  - Feedback: Religion mattered, but English colonization involved many actors who did not share one religious purpose.
- B. The Crown followed a complete imperial plan that had existed since the 1550s.
  - Feedback: England did not begin with a complete blueprint. Colonization developed through experiments, failures, and changing capacity.
- C. Religion, rivalry, profit, and migration opportunities converged as colonial capacity increased. **(best answer)**
  - Feedback: This is the strongest explanation because it combines several motives with the growing ability to sustain settlements.
- D. The defeat of the Spanish Armada immediately gave England uncontested control of the Atlantic.
  - Feedback: The Armada mattered, but it did not destroy Spanish power or make permanent settlement automatic.

**Why this answer is strongest:** This is the strongest explanation because it combines several motives with the growing ability to sustain settlements.

**Walk Me Through It**

1. Identify the outcome: England moved from limited Atlantic ventures toward durable colonies.
2. Reject answers that reduce a long process to one group, one event, or one national plan.
3. Separate motives from capacity. Wanting a colony did not mean England could sustain one.
4. Choose the answer that explains why several motives became effective at the same time.

**Transfer strategy:** For a multi-cause question, choose the explanation that shows how causes interacted instead of selecting a favorite cause.

**Source alignment:** English colonization resulted from the convergence of religion, rivalry, profit, and opportunity, combined with greater colonial capacity.

### ch02-explain-002 — Explain / consequence

Why did tobacco transform Virginia rather than simply make the colony profitable?

  - Feedback: Tobacco tied Virginia more closely to Atlantic markets. It did not create economic self-sufficiency.
- D. It made relations with Powhatan communities more stable by reducing competition for territory.
  - Feedback: Tobacco expansion increased pressure on Native land and made relations more dangerous.

**Why this answer is strongest:** This is the strongest answer because it traces the wider chain of consequences created by the crop.

**Walk Me Through It**

1. Start with the immediate result: tobacco became a profitable export.
2. Ask what profitable production required: fresh land and many workers.
3. Trace what those needs produced: expansion, bound labor, inequality, and conflict over territory.
4. Select the answer that explains the whole chain, not only the first step.

**Transfer strategy:** For a consequence question, trace the chain beyond the immediate effect and identify what changed in society and power.

**Source alignment:** Tobacco reorganized Virginia around land-intensive export production, bound labor, expansion, and conflict over Native land.
