## Historical cautions preserved

- England's colonization is presented as convergence and increasing capacity, not a single plan or an inevitable result of the Armada.
- Jamestown is not reduced to a morality tale about settlers who would not work.
- Tsenacommacah and Native nations are treated as existing political worlds, not empty land or background scenery.
- The development of racial slavery is treated as a process rather than an institution created instantly in 1619.
- Puritan religious freedom is distinguished from broader religious toleration.
- Representative institutions are not labeled modern democracy.
- Native alliances are not reduced to a simple English-versus-Indian conflict.
- Colonial self-government and the contraction of Indigenous sovereignty are connected rather than taught as separate stories.
